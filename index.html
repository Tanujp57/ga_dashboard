Georgia, 1960–2024

How Georgia turned red, one office at a time

For nearly a century after Reconstruction, Georgia was a one-party Democratic state, part of the "Solid South." The realignment started at the top of the ballot. In 1964, Georgia voted Republican for president for the first time ever. Senate and governor's races took much longer to follow.

Chapter 1 · President

1964: the top of the ticket breaks first

In 1960, John F. Kennedy carried Georgia by 25 points. Four years later, Barry Goldwater, who had voted against the Civil Rights Act of 1964, carried it by 8. That 33-point swing made Georgia a Republican state in a presidential race for the first time.

Georgia has leaned Republican for president ever since. The exceptions were segregationist George Wallace's third-party win in 1968, Georgia's own Jimmy Carter in 1976 and 1980, Bill Clinton's narrow plurality in 1992, and Joe Biden's 0.2-point win in 2020.

Chapter 2 · U.S. Senate

1980–2004: the Senate follows a generation later

Georgians kept sending Democrats to the Senate long after they started voting Republican for president. Through 1966, Republicans often didn't field a candidate at all. In 1980, Mack Mattingly narrowly beat longtime senator Herman Talmadge, but Democrats took the seat back in 1986.

The lasting flip came later. Paul Coverdell won in 1992, Saxby Chambliss beat Max Cleland in 2002, and Johnny Isakson's 2004 win gave Republicans both of Georgia's Senate seats. They held both until January 2021, when Jon Ossoff and Raphael Warnock won the runoffs.

Where a runoff decided the seat, the bar shows the runoff. Light blue bars mark races with no Republican nominee.
Chapter 3 · Governor

2002: the governor's office is the last to flip

Governor's races held out the longest. In 1966, Republican Bo Callaway actually won the most votes. No candidate won a majority, though, so under Georgia law the Democratic legislature chose the governor, and it picked Lester Maddox. Democrats then held the office for 36 more years, often by landslides.

By the 1990s, Democratic margins had shrunk to single digits. In 2002, Sonny Perdue unseated Roy Barnes, and Republicans have won every governor's race since. Still, Brian Kemp's 2018 margin was only 1.4 points.

Epilogue

A realignment from the top of the ballot down

Georgia's move to the Republicans took about four decades and reached each office in turn: the presidency in 1964, the Senate in 1980 (for good by 2004), and the governorship in 2002. For a generation, many Georgians voted Republican for president while still electing conservative Democrats at home.

The story isn't finished. Biden carried Georgia in 2020, Ossoff and Warnock won both Senate seats in 2021, and Trump won the state back in 2024. Today Georgia is one of the closest swing states in the country.
